Could other strategies improve the return?

In this particular period, none of the tested timing rules beat buying every January. The stronger levers are getting available money invested sooner, reinvesting dividends and avoiding high fees.

All comparisons below are fixed, price-only backtests ending 8 Oct 2026. The sliders above do not change this section. Amounts are US dollars.

What I would take from this

  • Invest money when it is available rather than waiting for a perfect dip. If money arrives monthly, monthly investing is practical; this test assumes it was available in January.
  • Check the actual fund’s ongoing fee, sales charge, tracking difference and dividend treatment. Lower costs keep more of the return, all else equal.
  • Use a schedule you can keep through a crash. A timing filter may reduce losses, but can sacrifice long-term wealth and adds trading work.
  • I have not added leverage, picked winning stocks after the fact or chosen the best trading day with hindsight. Those would distort this comparison or add risks the index data cannot measure.
These rules were evaluated on one historical window, not on unseen future data. Parameters were chosen for illustration, not optimised. An actual unit trust may have dealing cutoffs or execution delays, and its price will differ from the index. This is historical analysis, not a recommendation to switch funds or trade.

Assumptions

  • You buy $5,000 of the index on the first trading day of each year, at that day's closing price: 1997 through 2026. That is 30 payments.
  • The 2026 payment has been in for only about nine months, and the pot is valued at the 8 October 2026 close.
  • Base case is price only. The S&P 500 price index leaves out dividends, so real results with dividends reinvested would be higher. Fees and taxes, and the cost of converting currency from Singapore dollars, would lower them.
  • The annual return is the money-weighted rate, which accounts for each payment going in at a different time.
  • An actual unit trust may also charge a sales fee. None is included.
This is a look at the past, not advice and not a prediction. Past returns do not promise future ones. Source: S&P 500 daily closes from Yahoo Finance (index ^GSPC).
